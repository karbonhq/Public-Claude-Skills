## Purpose

This policy defines how [firm name] uses artificial intelligence (AI) in client-facing and internal work, and the guardrails that protect our clients, our staff, and our firm.

We treat AI like a member of staff: it is engaged, supervised, trained, and evaluated. The professional accountable for any client deliverable remains accountable, regardless of how much of that deliverable AI assisted with.

## The hard rules

1. **No client data — including any tax return information, account numbers, SSNs, financial figures, or identifiable client communications — into any consumer or personal-tier AI tool.** This includes ChatGPT (Free or Plus), Claude (Free), Gemini (personal), and Microsoft Copilot (personal). These tiers train on user prompts. That is a §7216, GLBA, and AICPA confidentiality risk.
2. **No autonomous AI delivery to clients.** Every AI-assisted deliverable is reviewed by a qualified preparer or reviewer before delivery.
3. **No AI-generated tax positions, audit opinions, or written tax advice without human verification of citations and conclusions.** Hallucinations are a due-care issue, not a vendor issue.
4. **No new AI vendor without vetting.** Vetting is owned by [name] and includes SOC 2 Type 2, encryption, training-data terms, sub-processors, data residency, and breach reporting.
5. **No AI use that has not been logged in the workpaper trail.** For any client deliverable: log the prompt summary, the model, and what changed in review.

## Compliance frame (in plain English)

- **§7216** — pasting client tax data into a tool that trains on prompts is potentially a federal misdemeanor.
- **GLBA / FTC Safeguards Rule** — every AI vendor sits inside our Written Information Security Plan (WISP). The Qualified Individual is [name].
- **AICPA Code of Conduct + revised SSTS** — AI is a "tool"; we engage, supervise, train, and evaluate it like staff.
- **Circular 230** — the preparer signing the return is accountable for AI-assisted advice.
- **ET 1.295 (audit firms)** — AI-generated content the firm subsequently audits creates a self-review threat; safeguards are documented per engagement.
- **E&O / professional liability** — engagement letters disclose AI use; the carrier has been informed.

## Incident response

If AI produces something incorrect, fabricated, or harmful in client work:

1. Stop delivery. Pull the deliverable back if already sent.
2. Notify the engagement partner and [name] within 24 hours.
3. Document what happened: prompt, model, output, what was caught, what was sent.
4. Determine client communication required.
5. Update the policy and the sanctioned-tool list if the incident exposes a new gap.

## Engagement-letter language

We disclose AI use to clients via the following clause in our engagement letters:

> "[Firm name] uses artificial intelligence tools to support our work, including drafting communications, summarizing documents, and supporting research. All AI-assisted work is reviewed by qualified professionals before delivery. We do not use any AI tool that uses client data for model training. By signing this engagement letter, you consent to our use of AI tools as described in our AI use policy, available on request."
