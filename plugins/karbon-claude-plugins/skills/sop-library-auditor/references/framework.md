# 16-Domain × 5-Layer SOP Framework

The taxonomy organizes every SOP an accounting/bookkeeping/CAS firm should consider into **5 layers** and **16 domains**. Layers describe the firm's operating model (what kind of work this is). Domains group SOPs by procedure type (what specifically gets done). Each domain contains specific SOPs the firm may or may not have, depending on size and service mix.

When classifying an existing SOP, prefer the **most specific domain** match. A document titled "Bookkeeping Manual" that covers reconciliations, AP, and AR is not one SOP — it's three. Split it into multiple inventory entries that point to the same source file (with a `dup` flag noted in the inventory).

## Layer A — Value Chain (Domains 1–3)

How the firm acquires, contracts with, and serves clients end-to-end. The "outside-in" view.

### Domain 1 — Client Lifecycle

Sales-to-onboarding-to-offboarding flow.

- 1.1 Lead intake & discovery call
- 1.2 Scoping questionnaire
- 1.3 Proposal & engagement letter (Ignition / GoProposal / native)
- 1.4 CRM-to-PM handoff
- 1.5 Welcome packet & kickoff meeting
- 1.6 Tech-stack provisioning (QBO/Xero, Karbon, Bill.com, Gusto, Ramp, Relay)
- 1.7 Chart-of-accounts setup / cleanup
- 1.8 Opening balance & historical catch-up
- 1.9 Client portal & secure file-share setup
- 1.10 Client cheat sheet creation
- 1.11 Communication SLA & meeting cadence
- 1.12 Scope-change request & price-change request
- 1.13 Pause/hold of services
- 1.14 Service-tier upgrade
- 1.15 Client termination notice
- 1.16 Data export & handoff (QBO/Xero file, payroll history, sales-tax registrations)
- 1.17 Access revocation & post-offboarding archive
- 1.18 Final invoice & retainer reconciliation

### Domain 2 — Document & Data Collection

Getting and managing the inputs that production work depends on.

- 2.1 PBC (Provided By Client) list management
- 2.2 Document collection cadence (Dext / Hubdoc / Bill.com inbox / portal)
- 2.3 Portal discipline & file-naming conventions
- 2.4 W-9 collection at vendor onboarding
- 2.5 Bank statement and credit-card statement intake
- 2.6 Payroll register intake
- 2.7 Receipt capture & matching
- 2.8 Client task chasing & escalation
- 2.9 Source-document retention & archival

### Domain 3 — Pricing & Billing

Money in. The firm's own AR.

- 3.1 Pricing & packaging policy
- 3.2 Tier definitions & service-level boundaries
- 3.3 Annual price review
- 3.4 Invoicing cadence (firm's own)
- 3.5 Fixed-fee / value-pricing administration
- 3.6 Subscription billing (Ignition)
- 3.7 Time-tracking policy & realization targets
- 3.8 Collections cadence for the firm itself
- 3.9 Change-order / scope-creep handling

## Layer B — Production (Domains 4–9)

The actual accounting/bookkeeping/CAS work — where the firm produces deliverables.

- 4.9 Vendor statement reconciliation
- 4.10 Customer setup & invoicing

### Domain 7 — Financial Reporting

Producing and delivering financial statements and management reports.

- 7.1 GAAP basis vs. cash/tax/OCBOA disclosure

## Layer D — Enablement (Domains 12–14)

The infrastructure that production work depends on.

### Domain 12 — Tech Stack & Integrations

Tools, integrations, and data flows.

- 12.1 Software stack standard (current inventory)
- 12.2 New-app evaluation & vendor security review
- 12.3 Integrations between core tools (QBO ↔ Karbon ↔ Bill.com ↔ Gusto, etc.)
- 12.4 Data-flow controls (where data crosses tools)
- 12.5 Karbon / PM-tool template authoring & versioning
- 12.6 Tool migrations (e.g., QBO → Intuit Accountant Suite)
- 12.7 Reporting-tool dashboard maintenance
- 12.8 Sandbox / test environment policy

### Domain 13 — People, Training & RACI

The humans.

- 13.1 Role descriptions
- 13.2 RACI per process (firm-level matrix referenced by individual SOPs)
- 13.3 Recruiting & interview scoring
- 13.4 New-hire onboarding (firm)
- 13.5 Role-based training paths (bookkeeper, senior, controller, manager)
- 13.6 CPE tracking & licensure
- 13.7 Performance review & career ladder
- 13.8 Training records
- 13.9 Knowledge transfer & shadow-and-correct procedures

### Domain 14 — Internal Firm Operations

Running the firm.

- 14.1 Firm overview / about-us / org chart
- 14.2 File management / drive structure (with legacy migration plan)
- 14.3 Backup + DR (3-2-1 with immutable copy) + annual tabletop exercise
- 14.4 Insurance summary (E&O, cyber, BOP)
- 14.5 Succession plan
- 14.6 Marketing & sales operations
- 14.7 Client NPS / feedback collection
- 14.8 Knowledge-management governance (SOP authoring, version control, retirement)

## Layer E — Governance (Domains 15–16)

Risk, compliance, and the meta-policies that govern everything else.

### Domain 15 — Risk, Compliance & Security

The non-negotiable layer.

- 15.1 WISP — Written Information Security Plan (FTC Safeguards + IRS Pub 4557)
- 15.2 Information security program — named coordinator, documented risk assessment, annual report
- 15.3 Access control & MFA policy
- 15.4 Encryption (at rest, in transit, AES-256)
- 15.5 Incident response & breach notification (24–48 hr IRS Stakeholder Liaison; state regimes)
- 15.6 Vendor / third-party security review
- 15.7 BYOD & remote-work policy
- 15.8 Data retention & destruction (state-specific & IRS)
- 15.9 §7216 consent procedure (third-party sharing / offshoring)
- 15.10 Annual independence / conflict-of-interest confirmation
- 15.11 Client acceptance & continuance
- 15.12 AML / KYB checks (UK / Canada)
- 15.13 Engagement risk scoring
- 15.14 SOC 2 readiness (when client-required)

### Domain 16 — AI & Agentic Workflows

The newest domain — and increasingly the most likely to be missing entirely.

- 16.1 AI usage policy (acceptable use, prohibitions, client-data restrictions)
- 16.2 Approved AI tools list (Karbon AI Agents, Aider, Claude, ChatGPT, Keeper, Digits, Intuit AI)
- 16.3 Prompt library governance
- 16.4 AI oversight tier definitions (`human_only` / `ai_assisted` / `ai_executed` / `ai_proposes` / `client_action`)
- 16.5 Engagement-letter AI consent language
- 16.6 Onboarding AI-consent disclosure
- 16.7 Model risk policy (which models, for what work, with what review)
- 16.8 AI execution logs & evals
- 16.9 Prohibited AI uses (regulatory attestation, conflict decisions, fraud escalation)
- 16.10 Vendor AI feature review (when a tool ships an AI feature, who reviews it before use)

## Layer rollup (for the dashboard)

The dashboard's heatmap shows the 16 domains in an 8-column grid (2 rows on mobile). Each cell carries its layer label as a tertiary tag. Layer-level rollups are computed but secondary — the domain-level view is the headline.

| Layer | Domains | Typical pattern |
|---|---|---|
| A — Value chain | 1, 2, 3 | Often partial — onboarding documented, offboarding informal |
| B — Production | 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | Strongest area for established firms; advisory typically weak |
| C — Quality | 10, 11 | Frequently missing in firms 4–15; the top hidden risk |
| D — Enablement | 12, 13, 14 | Tech stack often inventoried but integrations undocumented |
| E — Governance | 15, 16 | Universal compliance gaps; AI domain almost always missing |

## Edge cases in classification

**A document spans multiple domains.** Bookkeeping Manual covering reconciliations + AP + AR + JEs maps to Domain 4 (transaction processing) for the AP/AR sections AND Domain 5 (reconciliations) for the bank-rec section. Tag the inventory entry with `scope` to flag the scope-mismatch.
