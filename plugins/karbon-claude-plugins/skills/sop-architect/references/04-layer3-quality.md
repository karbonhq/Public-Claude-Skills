## Domain 10 — Quality control & review

### AICPA SQMS Nos. 1-3 — effective December 15, 2025

This is the most consequential governance change for accounting firms in years. SQMS reshapes every firm's quality system.

**SQMS No. 1 — A Firm's System of Quality Management** requires eight components:

1. Firm risk assessment
2. Governance and leadership
3. Ethical requirements
4. Acceptance and continuance
5. Engagement performance
6. Resources (human / technological / intellectual / service-provider)
7. Information and communication
8. Monitoring and remediation

**SQMS No. 2** governs the Engagement Quality Review (EQR) — rules on appointment, eligibility, cooling-off, objectivity, documentation.

**SAS 146** covers engagement-level QM under GAAS. **SSARS 26** mirrors it for SSARS engagements.

### Critical scope clarification for CAS — SSARS 27

**SSARS 27 (March 2025) clarifies that CAS work performed under CS 100 (Consulting Standards) is NOT subject to SSARSs when financial-statement preparation isn't the primary objective.**

Under CS 100, the practitioner relies on:

- Professional Competence
- Due Professional Care
- Planning and Supervision
- Sufficient Relevant Data

**However: CAS engagements still inherit firm-level SQMS obligations even when individual engagements fall under CS 100.** The standards inherit at firm level regardless of engagement type.

### Tiered review structure (standard pyramid)

```
Preparer (staff bookkeeper)
    ↓
Reviewer / Senior
    ↓
Manager
    ↓
Partner / EQR
```

### Four-eyes principle

Two individuals must approve before finalization. Required separations:

- Initiator ≠ approver
- Role-based access
- Approval queues
- Audit trails

For CAS, four-eyes is most commonly applied to:

- Journal entries above thresholds
- Payroll runs
- AP payment release
- Month-end financials before client delivery
- Year-end adjustments

### Self-review checklists (public-domain references)

- **FloQast Month-End Close Checklist** — Excel templates by former auditors
- **Karbon Bookkeeping Best Practice template** — 7 sections (bank statement collection, classification, suspense clearing, accruals, recs, financial statements, communication) with task owner / duration / due date per step

### Standard self-review items

- Bank/CC recs to GL
- Balance-sheet schedules tied to subledger
- P&L reasonableness vs. prior period
- Cutoff testing for AP/AR
- Payroll tie-out to provider
- Sales-tax liability roll-forward
- Fixed-asset additions vs. cap policy
- Intercompany eliminated
- "No plug" rule on suspense

### Engagement Quality Review for CAS

Apply on triggers (even where SQMS 2 doesn't strictly mandate):

- New client first-period close
- Engagement above $X annual fees (firm-defined threshold)
- Industry complexity (multi-entity, inventory, ASC 606 revenue recognition)
- Client preparing for a transaction or financing
- Regulated industries

### Error tracking

A written **Error/Issues Log** capturing:

- Date
- Client
- Preparer
- Reviewer
- Error category
- Root cause
- Remediation
- Training implication

**Boomer Consulting** promotes Lean Six Sigma-driven continuous improvement and offers the **Boomer Certified Process Improvement Leader (CPIL)** program in three levels.

### Common gaps in Domain 10

- No documented SQMS 1 risk assessment (now mandatory effective 12/15/2025)
- No EQR triggers defined
- Preparer = reviewer (control failure)
- No error log
- No formal monitoring/remediation cadence
- "Done = accurate" without independent verification

## Domain 11 — Workflow & practice management

### Capacity planning formulas

Synthesized from Lagerquist, rework.com 2026 Guide, Madras Accountancy:

- **Utilization** = billable hours ÷ net available hours
- **Target utilization:** 70-80% professional staff (50-65% partners, can reach 80-85% during build)
- **Net available hours** ≈ 1,750/year
- **Sensitivity:** every 5% utilization change drives 3-7% profit margin change

### Capacity forecast cadence

- **13-week rolling horizon**
- Flag <60% (pipeline gap) or >90% (burnout/miss risk)

### The hire / outsource / raise prices triad

When sustained >85% utilization with realization <85%, three options:

1. **Hire** — U.S. senior $95-125K all-in
2. **Outsource** — Outsourced senior $25-40K all-in (offshore)
3. **Raise prices** — 30% increases commonly result in <10% client loss
